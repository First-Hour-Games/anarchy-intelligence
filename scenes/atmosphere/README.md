# Forest apparition prototype

Run `starting_forest.tscn` and finish its opening dialogue. Automatic sightings are enabled: hat man becomes due every **30 seconds** of active gameplay, and the dog every **100 seconds**, each measured from scene entry or that shape's last successful automatic appearance. The clocks pause during dialogue, inspection, inventory, map, and pause. A due sighting waits for a suitable location, retrying once per second; hat man still requires a peripheral tree and the dog a clear path with the camera facing ahead. Only one figure appears at a time. If both are due, the dog appears first and hat man waits for it to finish. There is no accumulated backlog.

Hold **Alt** and press the main keyboard's **+/=** key or **numpad +** to trigger sightings manually for development, alternating between hat man and dog after each successful trigger. Shift is optional on the **+/=** key, so this is a two-key shortcut and also works while Shift is held for sprinting. Plus alone does not trigger a sighting. Manual triggers do not reset the automatic clocks, and automatic sightings do not change the manual sequence.

The `ForestApparitions` node controls the automatic intervals and `automatic_sightings_enabled`, along with distance, gaze duration, maximum appearance time, retreat speed, and `peek_body_shift` (the distance the upright figure slides out). The developer action is ignored by release builds and can also be switched off with `developer_trigger_enabled`; automatic sightings work independently of that developer action.

The hat man uses the 12–24% band from either side of the 4:3 gameplay area, preferring 18% in. The outer 12% is reserved for the curved TV border, and sightings stay below the upper HUD. He starts fully concealed against the measured bark edge, slides outward 36 cm over 0.55 seconds while standing straight, then slides back when watched or brought toward the center of the view. At full reveal, the bark edge crosses approximately the middle of his silhouette, exposing half his hat, torso, and legs. Bark cross-sections at four heights determine the concealment boundary, excluding transparent foliage cards and respecting mesh offsets, rotation, and scale. The boundary updates as the player moves; normal tree depth occlusion still applies. Sightings stay within 22 meters to keep the exposed portion readable.

The dog does not use tree locations. With the camera facing ahead, it starts outside the bottom-left view, rushes across and closer to the player's feet, and exits within 0.4 seconds. Its feet are grounded and the ears are framed near 86% down the gameplay view so the body stays mostly out of frame. The trajectory includes the player's horizontal velocity at trigger time, making the glimpse visible while walking or sprinting instead of leaving it behind the camera. It stays in world space and does not turn or reattach itself when the player changes direction. A quick downward look ends the effect immediately. Solid props along the moving trajectory block or end a pass.

Both figures use opaque black geometry with normal depth testing, no shadows, collision, AI, interaction, or damage. They use render layer 20, which the image-map preview camera excludes. The player's atmospheric post-processing still applies. Dialogue, inspection, map, inventory, and pause suppress both effects; only one exists at a time.

These are simple procedural prototype shapes. Trees without a usable continuous bark profile are skipped. `tree_paths` can restrict sightings to selected tree nodes with visible bark meshes. Carefully chosen trees remain useful for polished encounters before adding random or story-driven sightings.
